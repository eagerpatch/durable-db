'durable-db': minor

The outbox says which database recorded a message, works out its tenant from what the build applied, and no longer lets a message the sink cannot take hold back the rest. In 0.13 such a message was retried with the 100 oldest forever, and nothing behind it was delivered.

- **The envelope names its database and its counted attempts.** `OutboxEnvelope` has `database` — the generated class's `databaseName`; for a hand-written subclass without one, the name derived from its class name, else the class name — and `attempts`, how many of its failures counted toward `maxAttempts` so far. A global database's key `'global'` and a per-tenant tenant named `global` were the same string before, and a sink could not tell them apart.
- **The tenant comes from what the build applied.** The generated classes know their database's `instance` (`databaseInstance`) and the suffix the build gives their keys (`devEpochSuffix = applyDevEpoch('', name)`: `__dev_<epoch>` in dev, nothing in production). A global database's instance has no tenant, whatever its key; a per-tenant one's tenant is its key without exactly that suffix. So `envelope.tenantId` and `getTenantId()` answer a tenant named `global` as that tenant, and keep a tenant id that contains `__dev_` whole. A dev key of an earlier epoch (an object `db reset` left behind) loses its trailing `__dev_<epoch>`. `tenantOfInstanceKey(key, { instance, devEpochSuffix? })` works it out the same way.
- **In an action of a per-tenant object, `getTenantId()` answers the object's own tenant first.** The resolver an app installs at its worker module's top level runs in every object's isolate too, and one that reads a request threw inside an action. The object hands its tenant to the action's DoContext store (`DoContext.tenantId`), which wins for the action and what it calls. A global database's object, code outside any object and an object's alarm (where the sink runs, reading `envelope.tenantId`) ask the resolver as before.
- **What a sink answers.** Nothing: every envelope was delivered. Or `{ retry?, dead?, error? }` of the envelope objects it was handed: those in `retry` were not delivered, those in `dead` never can be, and every other one counts as delivered. A throw is a `retry` of every envelope, and so is an answer that names an envelope it was not handed, or one in both lists. The sink is handed a frozen copy of its list, typed `readonly OutboxEnvelope[]`, and envelopes are paired with their rows before the call. Each field of the answer is read once and each list copied once, by index, and a function's fields are read as an object's: nothing a sink does to its list or its answer makes an unsent message count as delivered.
- **A failure counts only in an alarm that delivered another message.** The alarm hands the sink the oldest messages due, by id, at most 100 in a call. What a call did not deliver is halved, and both halves are tried next in the same alarm, so a bad message ends up the one of its call not delivered while the rest are delivered: of 100 messages with one bad, the other 99 go in that alarm — unless the bad one is the oldest of the read and the sink fails every call that holds it: then it fails alone before anything was delivered and takes the sink for down, and the 99 go at the next try, 5 s later. A group that fails moves no clock and counts nothing. A message that fails alone — the one of its call not delivered — is retried on its own backoff (5 s, doubling, at most 5 minutes; the new `tries`, `next_attempt_at`), and the failure counts toward `maxAttempts` (the new `counted`) only when the sink delivered another message in the same alarm, in that call or earlier. A message answered in `{ dead }` is no delivery. Nothing about a failure is judged by other alarms, so an alarm in which the sink delivers nothing counts nothing.
- **When the sink is down.** A failure alone before anything was delivered in its alarm cannot be told from an outage: it counts nothing, and the sink is taken for down (`__outbox_sink.down_until`, `down_rounds`). Until its next try nothing is read or called, and a message recorded meanwhile sets no alarm of its own. The try (5 s, doubling, at most 5 minutes) hands the sink the newest message alone and, if the sink does not take it, the one tried or recorded longest ago alone (the new `tried_at`); one it does not take goes behind every message recorded or tried before it, its count and backoff as they were. The first try at which the sink takes one ends it, and the alarm goes on. So, over tries at which the sink takes nothing, every waiting message is handed alone by the try after as many tries as there are messages ahead of it; a message recorded meanwhile goes behind it (and is handed first, as the newest).
- **The cap and `__outbox_dead`.** A message the sink answers in `{ dead }`, or whose counted failures reach `maxAttempts` — `durableDb({ outbox: { queue | module, maxAttempts } })`, default 100, at least 2 — is moved to the object's `__outbox_dead` table and logged. It is never retried by itself and never dropped. An outage counts at most one failure a message — in the alarm it began in, if the sink delivered another message in that alarm — so it buries only a message that already had `maxAttempts` − 1. A sink's error is kept as text even when `String()` cannot convert it.
- **What it costs.** An alarm makes at most 16 sink calls. An outage costs the rest of the alarm it began in (at most 16 calls in all) and at most 8 in the next; then each try makes at most two calls and writes at most two outbox rows and the sink's row, however much waits. An alarm while the sink is down reads no outbox row, and a message recorded meanwhile adds no alarm and leaves each try within those two calls and two rows — though while only one message waited, its tries made one call. With a sink that always throws, over an hour: with 2 000 waiting, 17 alarms, 40 calls and 50 rows written; with a message recorded every 10 s, 17 alarms, 32 calls and 49 rows written besides the messages. The outbox has no index: each would be a billed row written per message.
- **What it cannot tell.** A message the sink never takes while it takes nothing else (a poison on a quiet object, or every message refused) looks like an outage: never counted, the sink tried at its rounds (to 5 minutes); a message recorded meanwhile waits for the next try and is handed first as the newest, and each such delivery can count the poison once. A refused message at the head of the read, before anything was delivered, takes the sink for down: the rest wait 5 s, and a round more, doubled, for each try at which the sink refuses both messages it hands — while one alarm's halving gets past the refused messages at the head. While more of them fill it (a sink relaying to two places, one of them down, refusing that one's messages and taking the rest), each try delivers only its newest message and the next alarm is taken down again by the head: the rest wait, with no bound in tries, until those refused messages leave the read by their own backoff, the cap, or the sink taking them — with a message recorded every 2 s, up to 22 minutes behind 300 refused messages, and most still waiting after two hours behind 1 000. A sink that stops part-way through an alarm (a spent quota, a flap) has that alarm's failures alone counted, at most one a message an alarm. A sink that throws on what it can never take, instead of answering `{ dead }`, has those messages found by halving, a few an alarm. A sink that answers success for what it did not deliver loses it.
- **The Queue sink packs by bytes.** `queueOutboxSink()` stays within 100 messages and 256 KB a `sendBatch`, a message's size its UTF-8 JSON plus the queue's 100 bytes, and sends each with `contentType: 'json'`. A message over 128 KB is answered in `{ dead }`. A failed `sendBatch` answers the envelopes it left unsent in `{ retry }`; the batches before it were delivered.
- **The upgrade.** An outbox 0.13 left gets the new columns once, by `ALTER TABLE`, and nothing is reset. Its `attempts` column stays as it was and is never read, so 0.13 still runs on the table after a rollback, and what it counted (each failure on every one of the 100 oldest) counts nothing here.
- **`destroyDatabase()`.** The outbox is created again after it; the next message was refused with "no such table: __outbox". A call still in flight when it runs settles nothing on the storage that replaced the one it read: the alarm checks the storage after every awaited sink call, and again where it goes on.
- `createTestDatabase()`'s envelopes carry `database` and `attempts` too (options `database`, default `'test'`, and `instance`, default the action's own database's), and its `outbox` option is handed a frozen list, as the sink is.

**What a consumer changes.** `tenantOfInstanceKey(key)` is `tenantOfInstanceKey(key, { instance, devEpochSuffix? })`; the one-argument call throws. Code that builds `OutboxEnvelope`s adds `database` and `attempts`. A sink that changes its list (`splice`, `sort`, `push`) copies it first. A sink that worked around the tenant or the database can read `envelope.tenantId` and `envelope.database`. A tenant resolver is no longer asked in an action of a per-tenant object, or in what the action calls. `createTestDatabase()` runs an action of a `global` database on the key `'global'`, its envelopes' `tenantId` null. A hand-written `SqliteDurableObject` subclass used as a global database's object sets `databaseInstance = 'global'`. Sinks that return nothing keep working unchanged.
